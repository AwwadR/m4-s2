# Comparative Analysis

## Our KPIs and Charts

### Rand’s KPI and Chart

I chose order value distribution by product category and visualized it using a boxplot. I selected this KPI because I wanted to understand how customer spending differs across categories, not only which category has the highest average. I felt this was important because it gives a clearer view of customer behavior across product categories.

My chart shows that Books has the highest typical order values, while Food & Beverage and Sports tend to have lower order values. It also shows that Electronics has a wider spread, which suggests greater variation in customer spending. I chose a boxplot because it clearly shows the median, spread, and outliers, which makes category comparisons more meaningful than relying on averages alone.

### Afrah’s KPI and Chart

Afrah chose **monthly revenue over six months** and visualized it using a **line chart**. She selected this KPI because she wanted to track how revenue changed over time and see whether the business was showing growth. A line chart was a suitable choice because it clearly shows the overall trend and makes monthly movement easy to follow.

Her chart shows an overall increase in revenue across the six-month period, with the highest value appearing at the end of the timeline. This suggests that the business performance improved over time. Afrah considered this KPI important because understanding revenue trends can help support decisions such as when to invest more in marketing or when to expand operations.

### Raneem’s KPI and Chart

Raneem chose to compare **user growth and revenue indices across cities** using a **horizontal grouped bar chart**. She selected this KPI because she wanted to compare how revenue and user growth relate across different cities. This helped highlight whether business growth is being driven more by monetization or by customer acquisition.

Her chart shows that revenue is higher than user growth in most cities, especially in **Amman**, where the gap is the largest. **Madaba** shows moderate growth, while **Irbid** has the lowest values. This suggests that monetization is strong overall, but user acquisition may not be growing at the same pace in all locations.

## Where We Agreed
We agreed that all three charts point to positive business performance, but they show that growth is happening in different ways. Afrah’s chart highlights overall revenue growth over time, Rand’s chart shows that spending behavior is stronger in some product categories than others, and Raneem’s chart shows that some cities are generating stronger revenue relative to user growth. Together, these charts confirm that the Amman Digital Market has strong revenue potential, but that performance is not evenly distributed across all dimensions. We also agreed that using different KPIs gave us a broader and more realistic understanding of the business than relying on only one chart.

## Where We Disagreed
Our main difference was in what each of us considered the most important perspective. Rand focused more on **customer spending behavior by category**, Afrah focused on the **overall revenue trend over time**, and Raneem focused on the **relationship between revenue and user growth across cities**. We also used different chart types because each KPI needed a different kind of comparison: a boxplot for distribution, a line chart for time trends, and grouped horizontal bars for comparing values across cities. Another difference was in interpretation. Afrah’s chart gives a more optimistic view of steady business growth, while Raneem’s chart raises a concern that revenue may be scaling faster than user acquisition in some cities. Rand’s chart adds another layer by showing that some product categories perform more strongly than others, which may help explain part of the overall revenue pattern.

## What We Learned
This comparison helped us realize that one KPI alone cannot explain the full business picture. Looking at the three charts together helped us connect overall revenue growth, category-level spending behavior, and city-level performance. After this discussion, we understood that each of our original analyses could be improved by adding more business context and by linking our findings more clearly to other aspects of performance. Overall, this activity showed us that different analytical choices can reveal different but equally important insights from the same dataset.
